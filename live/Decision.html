Instructions

Welcome.

This study will consist of five periods. At the end of the study, one of the five periods will be randomly selected with equal chance. Your payment when you complete the study consists of $2 plus the earnings of the selected period.

The Basics

In this study, you and {{ nr_others }} other participants are in an experiment. Each participant will eventually receive one of {{ players_per_group }} prizes. Each prize is worth money, but might be worth a different amount of money for each participant.

You and the other participants will each rank prizes in any order you wish. Then, the interface uses an allocation process that uses these rankings to allocate the prizes, one for each participant.

The allocation process attempts to give each participant a prize that they ranked higher rather than a prize that they ranked lower. However, this is not always possible, since the allocation process must take into account the rankings of all participants.

Prizes and Priorities

There are {{ players_per_group }} prizes: Two A prizes, two B prizes, one Prize C, one Prize D, one Prize E and one Prize F.

At the end of each round each prize will be assigned to one of the {{ players_per_group }} participants.

Participants belong to one of two groups:

  • Four participants belong to Group A.
  • Four participants belong to Group B.

You will know which group you belong to.

For participants in Group A, Prize A is the most valuable prize and Prize B is the second most valuable prize.

For participants in Group B, Prize B is the most valuable prize and Prize A is the second most valuable prize.

For prizes C, D, E and F, how much each prize is worth to a participant is completely uncorrelated with how much they are worth to others and whether the participant belongs to Group A or Group B. For example, just because C is more valuable to a participant than F does not mean that any other participant will also make more money from C than F. In other words, you cannot tell how much another participant values prizes C, D, E and F just from knowing how much you value these prizes.

For example, a Group A participant may see Prizes as in this table:

Prize A (2) B (2) D (1) F (1) E (1) C (1)
Value to You $15 $13 $7 $5 $3 $1

Under each prize A, B, C, D, E, or F, this participant from Group A can see how much money each prize would add to their earnings. Specifically, if this participant ends up receiving prize A, it adds $15 to their earnings. If this participant ends up receiving prize B, it adds $13 to their earnings. Any participant from Group A will value Prize A at $15 and prize B at $13, but the value of the next four prizes are specific to this participant and may be different to other participants. For instance, this participant adds $7 to their earnings if they end up receiving Prize D and so on.

The number between parentheses next to the letter indicating each prize lets you know how many prizes with that letter are available. Specifically, there are two A prizes available, two B prizes available, but only one C, one D, one E and one F.

We also show an example for a Group B participant that may see Prizes as in this table:

Prize B (2) A (2) F (1) E (1) C (1) D (1)
Value to You $15 $13 $7 $5 $3 $1

Any participant from Group B will value Prize B at $15 and prize A at $13, but the value of the next four prizes are specific to this participant and may be different to other participants.

You and the other participants cannot affect the monetary value of prizes.

Prize Priorities

Next, we explain what we call prize priorities.

Our computer will randomly order the {{ players_per_group }} participants in your group, including you, from 1st to {{ nr_players_ordinal }}. Everyone has an equally likely chance of being any number, and no one can affect their prize priority.

You will not know your prize priority unless you have the lowest priority within your group. That is, the participant with the lowest priority in Group A will know that they have the lowest priority in Group A and the participant with the lowest priority in Group B will know that they have the lowest priority in Group B.

Submit Your Ranking

Your task is to rank prizes A, B, C, D, E and F.

You can rank all prizes, but you do not have to.

You can rank each prize at most once.

For example, if you want to rank Prize A first, Prize B second, Prize C third, Prize D fourth, and rank no further prizes, you will select “A” followed by “B” followed by “C” followed by “D” and then click “Submit Ranking”.

The other participants will submit their own rankings. They do not know your ranking.

Your own ranking cannot affect the other participants' rankings.

Allocation Process

The interface uses the allocation process to assign prizes to participants.

How the allocation process works:

These details are important to learn: You may be able to apply your knowledge to make better decisions in deciding how many prizes you want to rank and in what order.

Some details may seem confusing at first. This is quite natural! But don't worry, things will become clearer along the way.

Overview of the Allocation Process

First, the process aims to match participants with prizes based on their rankings.

The first part of the allocation process works in the following way:

Round 1: The participant with the first prize priority takes the first turn. That participant will be assigned the prize they submitted first in their ranking. If this participant ranked no prizes, they remain unmatched for now.

If a prize was assigned to the participant with the first prize priority, that prize is excluded from the set of available prizes.

Round 2: The participant with the second prize priority takes the second turn. That participant will be assigned the top prize according to the ranking they submitted among the set of available prizes. If the participant has not ranked any of the available prizes, they remain unmatched for now.

The set of available prizes is adjusted to exclude any prizes assigned to participants in Rounds 1 and 2.

And so on.

At the end of this first part of the allocation process, some participants have received prizes and some participants may be unmatched. Likewise, some prizes were allocated to participants, and some prizes may remain available.

Second Allocation Process

Every unmatched participant, and only those, participate in the second allocation process, and all prizes that are still available, and only those, participate in the second allocation process.

The second part of the allocation process works as follows. Every participant who is unmatched randomly receives one of the prizes that are still available. In this second allocation process, your ranking of prizes is not taken into account. At the end of the second allocation process, every previously unmatched participant is matched to a prize, whether they ranked it or not.

Therefore, after the first and the second part of the allocation process, every participant is matched to a prize which determines their earnings for this round.

How you submit your ranking

The interface will inform you when it is your turn. It will show you the prizes that are still available.

Instead of submitting a ranking you will just make a choice amongst the available prizes. You can either select one of the available prizes and be matched to that prize, or you can select no prize and remain unmatched.

Summary

This study consists of five periods. In each period, how much each prize pays to you is randomly determined. Make sure that you read the table carefully.

At the end of the study, one of the five periods will be randomly selected with equal chance. Your payment when you complete the study consists of $2 plus the earnings of the selected period.
